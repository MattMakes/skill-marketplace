# Pacing: length first, then sessions, unlocks and content

Sources:
- Stone Librande, "One-Page Designs", GDC 2010: the timed whole-game storyboard, and "decide the
  game's length up front instead of discovering it after".
- Jesse Schell, *The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses*: the Lens of the Interest Curve.
- Tracy Fullerton, *Game Design Workshop*: scope and completeness for a small team.

## Decide the length up front (Librande)

Librande's Spore creature map puts time along the bottom axis. The team decided how long each
stage lasts before building it, instead of finding out at the end. A decided number lets you
split the game into sessions, count content and check scope. Write one number, not a range.

## The timed storyboard (Librande)

A storyboard of the whole game with time marks shows where the player should be, and when. It
works for the team, for producers and for marketing. Six to ten panels are enough: first
minute, first new element, first big payoff, midpoint turn, last new element, ending.

## The interest curve (Schell)

A good experience has peaks and valleys and ends on its highest peak. It opens with a hook,
drops to let the player settle, then rises in steps. The curve is fractal: a session has its own
curve inside the whole game's curve. Put new elements on the rising steps. A long flat stretch
means the player drifts away.

## Unlocks

Unlock a thing when the player needs it, not when it is built. A good unlock answers a question
the player already asked ("how do I carry more?"). Teach one new element per session.

## Content for one developer

Count every asset type: levels, story segments, characters, sounds, UI screens. Give each an hour
cost for one developer, including iteration. If the total is larger than the scope, cut content
or shorten the game. Do not keep both.
