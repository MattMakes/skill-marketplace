# Blend deck: proven combinations of the eight kinds of fun

A **blend** is 2–3 of LeBlanc's eight kinds of fun that reinforce each other through ONE shared
dynamic, so the same core loop produces all of them. The first kind is the **primary** aesthetic;
the others are **supporting**. Pick a blend from this deck; never invent a combination that is not
here. Each blend cites where the combination is proven. See `eight-kinds-of-fun.md` for primary vs
supporting and the fold-in rule.

Format (machine-parsed; keep it exactly): one `## <id>: <Name>` heading per blend, then the seven
bullet keys below in this order, one line each.

## team-under-pressure: Team under pressure
- kinds: Fellowship, Challenge, Sensation
- signature dynamic: Players shout, hand off and cover for each other under a clock, so every save is shared, tense and physical at once.
- loop pattern: A player grabs and passes an item → the shared goal advances or a task goes wrong → a loud, juicy cue shows who needs help → the team decides who covers the gap next.
- reference games: Overcooked, Moving Out, PlateUp!, Spaceteam
- source: Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, "MDA" (2004), dynamic models: challenge from time pressure, fellowship from winning conditions that are hard to achieve alone; Game Developer, "Game Design Deep Dive: Building truly cooperative play in Overcooked"
- pitfall: Fellowship lives only in a lobby or leaderboard while each player solves a private task, so the clock gives Challenge but nobody needs anyone.
- boundary-safe verbs: cook, deliver, repair, route, sort, stack

## party-performance: Party performance
- kinds: Fellowship, Expression, Challenge
- signature dynamic: One player performs a clue in their own style while the room races to read it, so the laugh, the self-expression and the guess happen in the same moment.
- loop pattern: A player performs a prompt → teammates guess aloud against a timer → the room reacts to the guess and the performance → the next player decides how to perform the next prompt.
- reference games: Charades, Pictionary, Drawful, Telestrations
- source: Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, "MDA" (2004): Charades is Fellowship, Expression, Challenge, and "Charades emphasizes Fellowship over Challenge"
- pitfall: Expression becomes a cosmetic avatar picker while the actual clue is a multiple-choice pick with no room for personal style.
- boundary-safe verbs: paint, compose, sculpt, photograph, negotiate

## flow-mastery: Flow mastery
- kinds: Challenge, Sensation
- signature dynamic: The player makes fast, precise inputs at the edge of their skill, and every input lands with crisp feedback that makes the rhythm itself feel good.
- loop pattern: The player places or moves under time pressure → the system resolves the move instantly → tight audio and visual feedback shows clean or sloppy → the player picks the next move a beat later.
- reference games: Tetris, Celeste, Super Hexagon, Rhythm Heaven
- source: Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, "MDA" (2004): Quake's core is Challenge and Sensation ("Quake provides Challenge as a main element of gameplay"); Jenova Chen, "Flow in Games", MFA thesis, USC (2006)
- pitfall: Sensation lives only in a victory screen or unlockable effects while the moment-to-moment input feels flat.
- boundary-safe verbs: stack, climb, glide, navigate, balance, route

## clever-solve: Clever solve
- kinds: Challenge, Discovery
- signature dynamic: The player experiments with the rules to find the one hidden idea that cracks the puzzle, so the struggle and the aha are the same act.
- loop pattern: The player tries a move → the rules respond in a way that reveals how they work → the board shows a new consequence → the player forms the next hypothesis to test.
- reference games: Baba Is You, The Witness, Snakebird, A Monster's Expedition
- source: Nicole Lazzaro, "Why We Play Games: Four Keys to More Emotion Without Story" (2004): hard fun (mastery) and easy fun (curiosity); genre analysis of rule-discovery puzzle games
- pitfall: Discovery lives only in new level art or a map reveal between puzzles while each puzzle is a known mechanic with harder numbers.
- boundary-safe verbs: untangle, decode, arrange, route, map, balance

## curious-expedition: Curious expedition
- kinds: Discovery, Narrative
- signature dynamic: The player follows their own questions through the world, and each place they reach answers one question with a piece of the story and raises the next.
- loop pattern: The player goes somewhere that looked odd → the world reveals a clue or a record → the clue joins the story in a log → the player picks the next open question to chase.
- reference games: Outer Wilds, Return of the Obra Dinn, Heaven's Vault, Sable
- source: Alex Beachum, "Sparking Curiosity-Driven Exploration Through Narrative in Outer Wilds", GDC Independent Games Summit (2021); Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, "MDA" (2004): The Sims and Final Fantasy both pair Discovery with Narrative
- pitfall: Narrative lives only in cutscenes between areas, so exploring finds collectibles that tell no story.
- boundary-safe verbs: map, decode, navigate, catalog, photograph, sail

## object-story: Story in objects
- kinds: Narrative, Submission, Discovery
- signature dynamic: The player calmly handles and places ordinary objects, and each object quietly tells part of a life story.
- loop pattern: The player takes out an object → the object shows its detail and where it could go → it settles into place with a soft cue and a story hint → the player picks the next object.
- reference games: Unpacking, Gone Home, Florence, Assemble with Care
- source: Wren Brier and Tim Dawson, Unpacking design talk, GDC (2022), "item progression is character progression" (reported by Game Developer, "How the developers of Unpacking told a story with 1,000 items")
- pitfall: The story lives in text between chapters while placing objects is a neutral chore that tells nothing.
- boundary-safe verbs: arrange, sort, restore, catalog, repair

## life-in-a-role: Life in a role
- kinds: Fantasy, Expression, Narrative
- signature dynamic: The player lives a daily life as someone else in their own way, and the characters around them react, so role-play, self-expression and small stories come from the same choices.
- loop pattern: The player chooses how their character acts or dresses a space → characters and the world react in fiction → a reaction shows a small story beat → the player decides what their character does next.
- reference games: The Sims, Animal Crossing: New Horizons, Spiritfarer, Cozy Grove
- source: Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, "MDA" (2004): The Sims is Discovery, Fantasy, Expression, Narrative
- pitfall: Expression lives only in a cosmetic shop while the role's daily actions are fixed, so the player dresses a character but never plays the role their way.
- boundary-safe verbs: tend, build, arrange, plant, cook

## job-fantasy: Job fantasy
- kinds: Fantasy, Challenge
- signature dynamic: The player does a specific trade under real constraints and gets better at it, so competence at the job is what makes them feel like the role.
- loop pattern: The player works an order with the trade's own tools → the order resolves by the trade's rules → the customer or result shows the quality of the work → the player picks the next order or tool.
- reference games: Potion Craft, Euro Truck Simulator 2, Cook Serve Delicious
- source: Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, "MDA" (2004): Quake and Final Fantasy both pair Fantasy with Challenge; genre analysis of job simulators
- pitfall: Fantasy lives only in the menu art and job title while the work is an abstract matching minigame that fits any fiction.
- boundary-safe verbs: brew, cook, deliver, repair, forecast, navigate

## embodied-rider: Embodied rider
- kinds: Fantasy, Sensation, Challenge
- signature dynamic: The player moves a body through a space with skill and style, so being the rider, the rush of motion and the difficulty of the line are one act.
- loop pattern: The player commits to a line or trick → physics resolves the body's motion → speed, sound and camera sell the landing or the bail → the player picks the next line.
- reference games: Tony Hawk's Pro Skater, Lonely Mountains: Downhill, Alto's Odyssey, OlliOlli World
- source: Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, "MDA" (2004): Quake's Challenge, Sensation and Fantasy without its combat core; genre analysis of action sports games
- pitfall: Fantasy lives only in outfit unlocks while the controls do not feel like the role's body.
- boundary-safe verbs: climb, glide, navigate, balance, sail

## sensory-drift: Sensory drift
- kinds: Sensation, Submission, Discovery
- signature dynamic: The player drifts through a responsive space with no fail state, and every movement makes the world bloom in sound and color and reveals a new corner.
- loop pattern: The player steers gently → the world answers the motion with color, sound and growth → the change is beautiful and shows a new place → the player drifts toward whatever caught their eye.
- reference games: Flower, Abzû, Proteus
- source: Jenova Chen, "Flow in Games", MFA thesis, USC (2006); Nicole Lazzaro, "Why We Play Games: Four Keys to More Emotion Without Story" (2004): altered states (relaxation) and easy fun (curiosity)
- pitfall: Submission turns into boredom because the movement gives no sensory reply, so the calm feels empty rather than soothing.
- boundary-safe verbs: glide, sail, grow, navigate, plant

## cozy-builder: Cozy builder
- kinds: Expression, Submission, Sensation
- signature dynamic: The player shapes a place with no goal or fail state, and every placement pops into being with delightful detail, so the player's taste becomes the whole game.
- loop pattern: The player places a piece → the system adapts it to its neighbors → a satisfying animation and sound show the new shape → the player decides where the place grows next.
- reference games: Townscaper, Tiny Glade, Dorfromantik, Minecraft (creative mode)
- source: Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, "MDA" (2004): Expression comes from dynamics that let players leave their mark, such as designing and changing worlds; genre analysis of toy builders
- pitfall: Expression lives only in a decoration unlock track while each placement is forced by a scoring rule.
- boundary-safe verbs: build, arrange, plant, paint, sculpt, weave

## shared-sandbox: Shared sandbox
- kinds: Expression, Fellowship, Discovery
- signature dynamic: Players build and leave marks in one shared world and find each other's creations, so making, sharing and discovering are the same trips.
- loop pattern: A player builds or changes something → the world keeps it for everyone → another player finds it and reacts → that player decides what to add in reply.
- reference games: Minecraft (creative mode), Animal Crossing: New Horizons, LittleBigPlanet, Dreams
- source: Quantic Foundry, Gamer Motivation Model (2015): the Creativity pair (Design and Discovery) in the Immersion-Creativity cluster; Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, "MDA" (2004), Expression dynamics
- pitfall: Fellowship lives only in a friends list while players build alone and never meet each other's work in play.
- boundary-safe verbs: build, dig, plant, trade, map, paint

## silent-companions: Silent companions
- kinds: Fellowship, Discovery, Sensation
- signature dynamic: Strangers travel together through a beautiful unknown place with only simple gestures, so help, wonder and the sensory world arrive in the same moment.
- loop pattern: A player moves or signals → the companion and the world respond → sound and light mark the shared discovery → the pair decides together where to go next.
- reference games: Journey, Sky: Children of the Light, Unravel Two
- source: Jenova Chen, "Designing Journey", GDC (2013): remove power and tasks so players care about each other
- pitfall: Fellowship becomes a chat window while the world never needs a second player.
- boundary-safe verbs: glide, climb, navigate, sail, rescue

## soothing-restore: Soothing restoration
- kinds: Submission, Sensation
- signature dynamic: The player makes steady, visible progress on a messy thing with no fail state, and each small stroke looks and sounds satisfying.
- loop pattern: The player cleans, fixes or sorts one patch → the thing changes state at once → a crisp sound and visual shows the patch is done → the player picks the next patch.
- reference games: PowerWash Simulator, A Little to the Left, Unpacking
- source: Nicole Lazzaro, "Why We Play Games: Four Keys to More Emotion Without Story" (2004): altered states (relaxation); genre analysis of restoration games
- pitfall: The satisfying feedback lives only in a completion screen while the stroke itself is silent and slow.
- boundary-safe verbs: restore, repair, sort, tend, weave, untangle

## daily-garden: Daily garden
- kinds: Submission, Expression
- signature dynamic: The player tends a place in a calm daily rhythm and chooses what to grow and where, so the routine slowly becomes a place that reflects them.
- loop pattern: The player waters, plants or harvests a plot → plants grow on a gentle schedule → the garden visibly changes and fills in → the player decides what to plant and where next.
- reference games: Stardew Valley, Animal Crossing: New Horizons, Viridi, Wylde Flowers
- source: Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, "MDA" (2004): Final Fantasy includes Expression and Submission; genre analysis of farming life sims
- pitfall: Expression lives only in a furniture catalog while the plots are a fixed grid that every player fills the same way.
- boundary-safe verbs: tend, plant, grow, cook, arrange, herd

## network-puzzle: Growing network
- kinds: Challenge, Expression
- signature dynamic: The player builds a network under growing load, and each player's solution looks and works differently, so surviving the pressure and making it your own are the same act.
- loop pattern: The player draws or reroutes a link → the load flows through the network → congestion or smooth flow shows where it strains → the player decides where the network grows next.
- reference games: Mini Metro, Mini Motorways, Islanders, Railbound
- source: genre analysis of network-building puzzle games
- pitfall: Expression lives only in color themes while there is one right layout, so the pressure pushes every player to the same network.
- boundary-safe verbs: route, build, deliver, map, weave
